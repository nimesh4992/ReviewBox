## The thesis (the "why now")

Three things are true at once:

1. **Reviews drive installs.** A 0.5★ rating bump can lift store conversion 15–30%.
   App store ranking algorithms weight rating velocity heavily.
2. **Reviews drive product.** Every crash, regression, and roadmap signal is in the
   review feed before it's in Sentry, Slack, or your PM tool.
3. **ASO and review ops feed each other.** Your top-converting keywords are the
   exact words your 5-star reviewers use. The bugs killing your rating are the same
   ones killing your impression-to-install rate.

Existing tools treat these as three separate problems with three separate price tags:

- **AppFollow / Sensor Tower / data.ai** — built for enterprise SOCs, priced like
  enterprise SOCs ($300–$2,000/mo), feel like enterprise SOCs.
- **AppTweak / Mobile Action** — pure ASO. Treat reviews as a side panel.
- **Zendesk / Intercom** — generic helpdesk. Have no idea what an app version is.

ReviewBox collapses all three jobs into one tool, runs AI on top of the workflow
(not bolted on), and charges $49–$199/month. **More for less, literally.**

## Five pillars

Every section of the website, every blog post, every ad should ladder up to one of
these.

### 1. Triage in seconds, not hours
AI sorts every incoming review by sentiment, priority, and issue tag the moment it
hits the store. Crash reports, billing complaints, feature requests — pre-sorted
before you open the app.

### 2. Reply at scale, on brand
Three-tier reply pipeline: templates → cache → AI. Most teams serve 60–80% of replies
without a single AI call. The remaining 20% draft in two seconds, in your tone, with
your knowledge base baked in.

### 3. ASO that actually listens
Keyword suggestions don't come from a black box — they come from the language your
real reviewers use. Surface the exact phrases your 5-star fans repeat and miss
nothing your 1-star detractors are screaming about.

### 4. Spot incidents before the store does
Rating spikes, crash clusters, billing storms — detected in real time, emailed before
the app store hides your rating from search results.

### 5. One workspace, two stores, every team
Google Play and Apple App Store in the same queue. Same templates, same automations,
same ASO data. Loop in product, support, and exec stakeholders without buying three
seats elsewhere.

## Voice and tone

We sound like a sharp friend who builds shipped product. Not a vendor. Not a
thought leader.

**Do**
- Lead with the outcome, not the feature.
- Make strong claims. Back them with a number.
- Write at 8th-grade reading level. Re-read everything; cut every word you can.
- Use the verbs: ship, triage, reply, save, spot, surface, climb.
- Reference real workflows ("Monday release → Tuesday alert → Wednesday patch").
- Be specific about prices and limits. Vagueness reads as evasion.

**Don't**
- "Leverage" anything.
- "Empower" anyone.
- Talk about "synergy", "ecosystems", or "unlocking value".
- Use stock phrases: "next-gen", "best-in-class", "world's leading", "AI-powered" as
  a standalone descriptor.
- Hedge with "designed to help you potentially..."
- Imitate Apple's voice unless we're sure we can do it well. Borrow energy, not phrasing.

**Headline framework**
> [Specific outcome] in [specific time]. Without [the dumb thing the old way].

Examples:
- *Triage 1,000 reviews in 2 minutes. Without spreadsheets.*
- *Reply to every review in 10 minutes a week. Without hiring.*
- *Catch a crash spike in 4 hours. Without a paging tool.*
